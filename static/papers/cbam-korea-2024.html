Does the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) Affect Korean Firms' Stock Prices?

Chung-Won Woo (Changwon National University) & Yong-Hyun Kwon (Chungbuk National University)
Review of Accounting and Policy Studies, 29(4), 131–169, 2024 · DOI: 10.21737/RAPS.2024.11.29.4.131

TL;DR — First event study of the EU CBAM's impact on Korean firm value. Around the December 13, 2022 final CBAM agreement, Korean listed firms with higher carbon risk — larger greenhouse-gas emissions, energy consumption, and certified emissions — experienced significantly more negative abnormal returns (CARs/BHARs). The market priced CBAM in over the medium to long term, not as a one-day shock.

Key findings

1. Carbon risk predicted value loss. The higher a firm's carbon risk (GHG emissions, energy consumption, certified emissions), the more negative its stock price reaction to the CBAM announcement.
2. The effect was medium-to-long-term. Cross-border environmental regulation was absorbed gradually by the Korean stock market — evidence that investors treat CBAM as a structural cost, not transient news.
3. Policy implication. Carbon exposure is now a valuation issue, not only a compliance issue. Policymakers should prepare support measures for carbon-intensive export manufacturers ahead of stricter cross-border regimes.

Method in one line

Event study (Cumulative Abnormal Returns and Buy-and-Hold Abnormal Returns) around the final EU CBAM agreement of December 13, 2022, followed by cross-sectional regressions of abnormal returns on firm-level carbon-risk measures.

국문 요약

EU 탄소국경조정제도(CBAM) 최종안 합의(2022.12.13)에 대한 한국 상장기업의 주가 반응을 분석한 최초의 이벤트스터디. 온실가스 배출량·에너지 사용량·배출권 보유량이 큰 탄소위험 상위 기업일수록 기업가치 하락이 유의하게 컸으며, 그 영향은 단기 충격이 아니라 중장기에 걸쳐 주가에 반영되었다. 탄소배출 정보는 이제 환경 이슈가 아니라 기업가치 이슈라는 실증 근거.

FAQ

Q. Which sectors are most affected?
Carbon-intensive export manufacturing — steel, machinery, and other CBAM-covered industries central to Korea's export economy.

Q. What data measure "carbon risk"?
Firm-level greenhouse-gas emissions, energy consumption, and certified emissions of Korean listed firms.

Q. How does this relate to other evidence?
Consistent with Qing, Li, Dagestani, Woo & Zhong (2024, Journal of Cleaner Production): firm-level climate exposure erodes financial and energy performance, mitigated by CEOs' green experience.
